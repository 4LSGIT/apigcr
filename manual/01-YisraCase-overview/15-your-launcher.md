# Your own launcher: All, Ctrl+K, Home and the sidebar, keyboard shortcuts, and your own buttons

The **launcher** is every way into a feature: the sidebar, the Home tiles, the
More Features and Admin grids, your firm's own **pages**, the **All** tab and
the **Ctrl+K** search. Your firm sets it up for everyone. On top of that, you
can arrange **your own Home tiles and your own sidebar**, give features
**keyboard shortcuts** of your own, and make **buttons of your own** (a saved
view, a tool, a link to another website, a **list** that holds several, or a
**page** of your own, like More Features). Nobody else sees your arrangement,
your shortcuts or your buttons, and all three are saved on your account, so
they follow you to any computer you sign in on.

---

## All and Ctrl+K: every feature, always

**All**, the last item in the sidebar and the small grid icon under the Home
tiles, lists every feature your account has, with a search box on top. **Ctrl+K** (**⌘K** on a
Mac) opens the same search from anywhere. See *Finding any feature* in the
[introduction](01-introduction.md).

Whatever you hide or move, **All and Ctrl+K still list everything you have**.
The All icon under the Home tiles can't be hidden, and neither can Ctrl+K, so
there is always a way to find a feature and to undo a change. The sidebar's
All entry is an ordinary item: you may hide it there like any other.

## Customizing Home and the sidebar

Open **All** and press **Customize my launcher** (under the search box). The
editor opens over the app: a card on a computer, the whole screen on a phone.
It starts from your layout as it is saved right now, so a change you saved
from another browser tab or computer is already there (your Home and sidebar
catch up to it as the editor opens). If it can't load your layout, it says
*Couldn't load your current layout; try again* beside the button and doesn't
open.

The editor has five tabs at the top: **Home** and **Sidebar**, described
here, **Firm pages** (see [Your firm's pages](#your-firms-pages) below; it
shows only when your firm has pages you can see), **Keyboard shortcuts** (see
[Keyboard shortcuts](#keyboard-shortcuts) below) and **My buttons** (see
[My buttons](#my-buttons) below). A dot on a tab means it has unsaved
changes.

**Home** has two sections: **Home**, the tiles, and **Home's small
buttons**, the row of small round buttons under them (up to 12). **Sidebar**
has one. Each section lists that surface exactly as you see it now, top to
bottom: features with their icon and name, **Row break** rows on Home, and
section headings (like *Records*) on the sidebar. Each row has buttons on the
right:

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **↑ / ↓** | Moves the row up or down one place. On Home, the order is the tile order, left to right and row by row. |
| **Eye with a slash** (Hide) | On a standard item: takes its shortcut off this surface. |
| **×** (Remove) | On something you added (a shortcut, a row break, a section label): takes it out. |
| **Lock** (greyed) | Your firm locked this item: you can move it, but not hide it. |

Some rows carry a small tag: **Added** (a shortcut you added), **Firm** (one
of your firm's own items) or **Locked**.

A section heading on the sidebar is edited in place: click into its box and
type. A heading is plain text, 1 to 40 characters.

### Adding

- **Add a shortcut** opens a list of every feature you have that isn't on
  that surface yet, grouped the way All groups them, with a search box. Pick
  one and it goes to the end of the list; move it with the arrows. Anything
  can go on Home or the sidebar: a More Features or Admin panel, a firm item,
  a sidebar feature on Home, or one of the **More actions** (My Tasks, Log
  out, [Code](#code-running-javascript)), which have no tile or sidebar item of their own until you add
  one. A shortcut does exactly what the feature does anywhere else. A More
  panel pinned to Home, for example, opens More Features with that panel
  showing.
- **Add a small button** (Home's small buttons) puts any feature in the row
  of small round buttons under your Home tiles, the way Casey's paw sits under
  More Features. A small button shows only its icon; hold the pointer over it
  for its name. **All** is always in this row: you can move it, but not hide
  it, so there is always a way back to every feature.
- **Add a row break** (Home) starts a new row of tiles. Without a break, a row
  holds as many tiles as fit across the screen: the standard Home layout
  has a break after every three tiles.
- **Add a section label** (sidebar) adds a heading. Type it and press
  **Add** or **Enter**.

## Hide versus remove

- **Hide** is for the standard items, the ones a surface has by default:
  your firm's arrangement of it, if your firm has arranged it (see
  [What your firm controls](#what-your-firm-controls)), otherwise the app's.
  Hiding one takes it off **that surface only**: hiding Billing from the
  sidebar leaves its Home tile alone. Hidden items are listed under **Hidden
  on this surface** at the end of the section, each with a **Show** button
  that puts it back in its usual place.
- **Remove** is for what you added yourself. It's simply gone; you can add it
  again from **Add a shortcut**.
- Neither one takes a feature away from you. It's still in All and Ctrl+K,
  and it still opens from anywhere else it appears.

When YisraCase or your firm adds a new feature to Home or the sidebar, it
shows up for you too, next to the item it sits beside by default, even if
you've rearranged that surface. You can then move it or hide it like any other.

## Saving and cancelling

- **Your buttons save themselves.** On **My buttons**, adding a button (or
  changing, moving or deleting one) is saved the moment you do it: a short
  note says *Added “…”*, and the editor stays open. See [My buttons](#my-buttons).
- **Everything else waits for Save.** One Save covers the other tabs, and a
  page's tiles: it updates your Home, your sidebar and your keyboard shortcuts
  straight away, with no reload, and closes the editor. The status
  line at the bottom says *Unsaved changes* or *No changes*; Save is greyed
  out while there is nothing to save.
- **Cancel**, the **×** at the top, **Esc** and a click outside the card all
  close the editor. With unsaved changes they ask first, and so does
  reloading the page.
- If a save is refused, the reason shows at the top of the editor, and under
  the section it is about. Your changes stay in the editor; fix them, or
  Cancel. When more than one tab had changes, each is saved on its own, your
  buttons first: the message says which were saved and which weren't, the
  ones that were saved are done (their tabs lose their dot), and the others
  keep your changes for another try. If your buttons don't save, a Home,
  sidebar or shortcut change that uses a **new** button isn't sent either (it
  would point at a button that doesn't exist yet); it waits for your next Save.
- If you have YisraCase open in more than one browser tab, the others pick up
  your arrangement the next time they load, or when you open the editor there.
  If the editor is open in two tabs at once and you save from both, the later
  save replaces the earlier one.
- While a save is on its way, the editor takes no further changes.

## Your firm's pages

Your firm can make pages of its own: tabs of tiles like More Features (see
[Firm pages](../05-Subsystems/14-firm-launcher.md#firm-pages)). The firm
arranges them for everyone, and you can arrange them **for yourself** on the
**Firm pages** tab. It has one block per page you can see, each with its
**Tiles** and its **Small buttons**, and works like Home:

- The firm's tiles are your standard items: **Hide** takes one off the page for
  you (it waits under **Hidden on this surface**, with **Show**), and a tile the
  firm locked can be moved but not hidden.
- **↑ / ↓** move a tile, **Add a tile** puts anything you have on the page,
  **Add a row break** starts a new row, and **×** takes out what you added.
- **Reset to the firm's** puts that page back the way your firm arranged it
  (when you Save).
- When the firm changes a page later, what it adds shows up for you too, next
  to its neighbour; your own arrangement stays.

Nobody else sees your arrangement of a firm page. Hiding a tile there only
takes it off that page: it is still in All and Ctrl+K.

## Reset

**Reset Home to default**, **Reset small buttons to default** and **Reset
sidebar to default** each put **one** section back to the standard layout:
your firm's arrangement of it, or the app's if your firm hasn't arranged it.
They ask first, and take effect when you Save. The other sections keep your
arrangement.

## Keyboard shortcuts

A keyboard shortcut opens a feature from anywhere in YisraCase with a key
combination you choose: **⌃⌘T** (Control + Command + T) on a Mac, say, or
**Ctrl+Alt+T** on Windows, for Tasks. It works wherever you are in the app,
even while you are typing in a field or inside a page like a case, and does
exactly what clicking the feature does. The **Ctrl+K** search shows each
feature's shortcut beside its name.

### Adding one

1. Open **All**, press **Customize my launcher**, and pick the **Keyboard
   shortcuts** tab.
2. Press **Add a keyboard shortcut** and find the feature (type part of its
   name, or pick it from the list). Any feature you have can get one,
   including More Features and Admin panels and your firm's own items.
3. Click into the **keys** box and press the combination. The box shows what
   you pressed and says straight away whether it is free, taken or blocked.
   **Backspace** clears it.
4. Press **Add shortcut** (or **Enter**), then **Save**.

The list shows each shortcut with its feature. **×** removes one. A feature
can have more than one shortcut, but a combination can open only one feature:
the editor says which feature already has it.

### Which combinations you can't use, and why

- **A combination needs Ctrl, ⌘ (Command) or Alt (⌥ Option).** Without one,
  it would go off while you type. Shift alone doesn't count.
- **Ctrl+K and ⌘K** open the search, so they can't be shortcuts.
- **Ctrl or ⌘ with just one key** (⌘T, Ctrl+W, ⌘C, Ctrl+F…) belongs to the
  browser or to typing: new tab, close, reload, the address bar, copy, paste,
  undo, find, print. Some of those the browser never lets a page have at all.
  Add a second modifier instead: the editor suggests **⌃⌘** plus your key on a
  Mac and **Ctrl+Alt** plus your key elsewhere.
- **Alt (⌥) on its own** types special characters on a Mac (⌥E makes é) and
  opens the browser's menus on Windows.
- **Tab and Esc** stay free for moving between fields and closing dialogs.
- A few combinations the computer itself uses: for example ⌃⌘F (full screen),
  ⌃⌘Q (lock screen) and ⌃⌘Space (emoji) on a Mac, ⌘⌥I and its neighbours (the
  browser's developer tools), and Ctrl+Alt+Delete on Windows.

The editor names the reason whenever it refuses a combination.

**A warning, not a block: Ctrl+Alt on Windows.** On keyboards with an
**AltGr** key, which most non-US Windows layouts have, Ctrl+Alt with a key can
type a character such as € or @. The editor warns you when you pick a
Ctrl+Alt combination. If typing that character stops working in YisraCase,
pick another combination.

### Things to know

- **Shortcuts follow you.** They are saved on your account, so they work on
  any computer you sign in on. A shortcut that uses ⌘ needs a Mac keyboard;
  if you also use Windows, Ctrl+Alt combinations work on both.
- **A shortcut to something you can't see does nothing.** If your firm hides
  the feature, or it isn't available to your account, pressing the shortcut
  does nothing at all. The editor lists it as **Unavailable** and keeps it,
  so it works again if the feature comes back; remove it with **×** if you
  don't want it.
- **Shortcuts need a keyboard.** On a phone you can see and remove them in the
  editor, but not press them.
- They don't fire while a dialog is open, while this editor is open, or before
  you sign in. An editor that uses a combination for itself (an email editor's
  own shortcuts, for example) keeps it.
- Holding a shortcut down opens the feature once.

## My buttons

Your own buttons, besides the features and your firm's items: a saved view
you use every day, a tool, the court's website, a list of several things, a
page of your own. Make up to **50**. Only you see them, and they are saved on
your account like the rest of this page.

### What a button can open

| Kind | What it opens | How |
|---|---|---|
| **A saved view** | One of the firm's saved views (picked from a list) | Over the app, in a window you close with **×** or **Esc**. Pick **Large** (the usual) or **Compact** (a small card). Or as **a tab** of its own: see [Buttons that open as a tab](#buttons-that-open-as-a-tab). |
| **A tool** | One of the firm's live tools (picked from a list) | The same way: over the app, Large or Compact, or as a tab. |
| **A link to a website** | Any secure address (`https://…`), or a page of YisraCase itself (`/…`) | **Always in a new browser window**, never inside YisraCase. |
| **A list** | A small menu of up to 30 other items: features, your firm's items, your other buttons | Beside its tile or sidebar row; pick an item to open it. See [Lists](#lists). |
| **A page** | A tab of your own, like More Features: big tiles for anything you have, and a row of small buttons under them | As a main tab, from its tile, its sidebar row, All, Ctrl+K or a shortcut. See [Pages](#pages). |

**Why links open in a new window.** Another website isn't part of YisraCase:
shown inside the app, it would look like YisraCase while being somebody
else's page, and many sites refuse to be shown inside another one anyway. A
new window makes plain where you are. For the same reason a link must be
`https://`, and it can't point at a page that only works inside YisraCase
(a tool's or a saved view's own page): make a **Tool** or **Saved view**
button for those.

### Making one

1. Open **All**, press **Customize my launcher**, and pick the **My buttons**
   tab.
2. Press **Add a button** and fill in:
   - **Name**: what the button says, 1 to 40 characters;
   - **Icon**: press **Choose…** and pick one (see
     [Choosing an icon](#choosing-an-icon)); the icon and its name show
     beside the button;
   - **Opens**: a saved view, a tool, a link, a list or a page; then pick the
     view or the tool from the list (and where it **Opens in**: a dialog, large
     or compact, or a tab), type the address, fill the list, or — for a page —
     nothing more here: its tiles are arranged from its row (see
     [Pages](#pages)).
3. Press **Add button**. That saves it: there is nothing more to press.

Each button in the list shows its icon, its name, what it opens (*Saved view ·
Leads*, *Link · ecf.example.gov*), and where you have put it (*On Home*, *On
sidebar*, its keyboard shortcut). **↑ / ↓** set their order, which is their
order in All and the search. The **pen** edits one, the **bin** deletes it.
Each of these is saved as you do it. If a save is refused, the editor says why
at the top, the button stays in the list marked **New**, and **Save** tries
again.

If the lists of tools and saved views can't be loaded, the tab says so: link
buttons still work, and the lists come back the next time you open the
editor.

### Choosing an icon

**Choose…** opens the icon picker: every free
[Font Awesome](https://fontawesome.com/icons) icon, about 2,000 of them, in
three families: **Solid** (filled), **Regular** (outlined; a smaller set) and
**Brands** (company logos, such as GitHub or Dropbox).

- **Search**: type part of a name or a related word (`calendar`, `money`,
  `github`). Older Font Awesome names find their new icon too (`home` finds
  *house*).
- **All / Solid / Regular / Brands** narrow the list to one family.
- The icon you have now is outlined, and is named at the top.
- Click an icon to pick it. Point at one (or move to it) to see its name.
- Keys: **↓** from the search box moves into the icons; the arrow keys,
  **Home** and **End** move around; **Enter** picks; **Enter** in the search
  box picks the first match; **Esc** or **Cancel** closes it and keeps the icon
  you had.

The same picker chooses the icon of your firm's items
([Firm launcher](../05-Subsystems/14-firm-launcher.md)).

### Putting a button on Home or the sidebar, and giving it a shortcut

A button you make is in **All** and the **Ctrl+K** search straight away, under
**Mine**. To have it on your Home or sidebar, press **Add to Home** or **Add to
sidebar** under it in the list (or use **Add a shortcut** on the Home or Sidebar
tab); then arrange it there like anything else. On the **Keyboard shortcuts**
tab you can give it a key combination of its own. Putting it somewhere and
giving it a shortcut are arranging, so they wait for **Save** like any other
change on those tabs.

### Deleting a button

The bin deletes a button. If it is on your Home or sidebar, on one of your
pages, in one of your lists, or has a keyboard shortcut, the editor says so
and asks first; deleting it takes it off those too, so nothing is left
pointing at it. Deleting a page takes its own tiles with it. The delete is
saved at once. If you had other unsaved changes on the tab it also touches
(say, you had just moved a Home tile), that part waits for your **Save**, so
the delete never saves something you hadn't finished.

### Things to know

- **Your firm's settings don't reach your buttons.** The firm's hides,
  renames and **Who sees what** apply to the app's features and the firm's
  items; your own buttons are yours. A button is a shortcut, not a key: what
  the tool or view behind it lets you do is still decided by your role.
- A button's internal name (its id) is made from the name you give it and
  never changes once saved, so renaming a saved button keeps its place on
  your Home and its shortcut. A deleted button's id is never used again for
  you.

### Buttons that open as a tab

A saved view or tool button can open as **a tab** instead of over the app:
set **Opens in** to **A tab**. It then works like the app's own tabs: it fills
the screen beside the sidebar, its name and icon show at the top, and its
sidebar row is lit while you are on it. Open a case and come back, and it is
where you left it.

- **Up to 3 stay open.** The pages of the last three button tabs you opened
  stay loaded, so switching between them keeps your place. Opening a fourth
  closes the one you used least recently (the first time, a note says which);
  it loads again, from the start, when you next open it. You can have as many
  tab buttons as you like; only three are kept open at once.
- **A reload starts on Home.** The address bar never names a button tab
  (it would carry your button's name), so reloading the page while on one
  brings you back to Home; the button is still there.
- A link never opens as a tab: another website always opens in a new window
  (see above).

### The custom tab

There is no custom tab (**Settings → My Custom Tab**) any more. Each one became
a button of its owner's, in the same places on Home and the sidebar: a saved
view or a tool opens as a tab, as before; another page is a link or the app's
own tile for it. The button works like any other: rename it, move it, give it
a shortcut, or put it in a list. **Calendar** took the custom tab's place in
the shipped Home. If you had already arranged your Home yourself, it is added
to yours too, after **Log** (or the nearest tile before it that you kept);
take it off like any tile if you don't want it.

## Lists

A **list** is a button that holds other items: put the five things you open
every morning in one, give it a single tile on your Home or a single row on
your sidebar, and open the rest from there as a small menu. (Lists were called
*drawers* until October 2026; nothing but the name changed.) It is one of your
buttons, so it counts toward the 50, is listed under **Mine** in All and the
search, and can go on Home, on the sidebar, on a page, and get a keyboard
shortcut like any other.

### Making one

1. On the **My buttons** tab, press **Add a button**, give it a **Name** and an
   **Icon**, and set **Opens** to **A list**.
2. Press **Add to the list** and pick from the picker (type part of a name to
   find it). Anything you have can go in: a feature, a More Features or Admin
   panel, one of your firm's items, or one of your own buttons, even one you
   made a minute ago. The picker stays open, so you can pick
   several in a row; **Done** closes it.
3. **↑ / ↓** set the order the list shows them in, and **×** takes one out.
   A list holds up to **30** items.
4. Press **Add button** (that saves it). Put it on your Home or sidebar with
   **Add to Home** / **Add to sidebar**, as with any button.

In the list of buttons, a list says how many items it holds (*List · 4
items*), and each button inside one is tagged with the list's name.

### Opening it

- Click its tile or sidebar row: a small menu opens right beside it. On a
  phone it slides up from the bottom of the screen.
- From **All**, it opens under the tile you clicked; from the **Ctrl+K**
  search or its keyboard shortcut, near the top of the screen.
- Click an item to open it; the menu closes as it does.
- **Esc**, the **×**, or a click anywhere outside closes it, and so does a
  second click on its tile. With the keyboard, **↑ / ↓** (and **Home** /
  **End**) move between the items and **Enter** opens one.
- Only one list is open at a time. It doesn't open while a dialog or the
  **Customize my launcher** editor is open.

### Things to know

- **A list only shows what you can open right now.** If your firm hides a
  feature you put in a list, or it isn't available to your account, the list
  simply leaves it out (the editor still shows it, tagged **Unavailable**, so
  it comes back if the feature does). A list with nothing you can open says
  *Nothing here you can open*.
- **One level deep.** A list can't hold another list, and a button that is
  inside a list can't be turned into one; take it out of that list first.
- Deleting a button takes it out of every list that held it (see
  [Deleting a button](#deleting-a-button)).

## Pages

A **page** is a tab of your own that works like **More Features**: a heading,
big tiles, and a row of small round buttons under them. Put the panels you
live in — Automations, Users, a saved view that opens as a tab, a list — on
one page and open them from there. It is one of your buttons, so it counts
toward the 50, is listed under **Mine** in All and the search, and can go on
Home, on the sidebar, and get a keyboard shortcut like any other.

### Making one

1. On the **My buttons** tab, press **Add a button**, give it a **Name** and an
   **Icon**, and set **Opens** to **A page**. Press **Add button**.
2. Press **Arrange…** on its row. Two lists open under the buttons: **Tiles**
   and **Small buttons**.
   - **Add a tile** opens the same picker as Home's: a feature, a More
     Features or Admin panel, one of your firm's items (or firm pages), or
     one of your own buttons. A page can't hold itself.
   - **Add a row break** starts a new row of tiles. **↑ / ↓** move a row, **×**
     takes it out. Up to 100 entries.
   - **Add a small button** fills the row under the tiles — up to 12, like the
     All icon under your Home tiles.
3. Press **Done**, then **Save**. The page appears at once, with no reload.
   Put it on your Home or sidebar with **Add to Home** / **Add to sidebar**.

A button on one of your pages is tagged with the page's name in the list of
buttons (*On “My desk”*).

### Using it

- Its tile, its sidebar row (lit while you are on the page), All, Ctrl+K or a
  keyboard shortcut opens it as a main tab.
- A **panel** tile (Automations, Users, Manuals…) opens that panel **on the
  page itself**, with the page's own strip of pills across the top — exactly
  as More Features does. The panel is the same one More Features shows:
  switch from the page to More Features and back and you are looking at the
  same Automations, with what you typed still there. Each page remembers
  which panel it had open.
- Any other tile opens the feature where it always opens: a tab, a dialog, a
  list's menu, a link in a new window.
- **Reloading the browser while you are on a page brings you back to Home.**
  The address bar never names your page (your pages are yours alone); More
  Features and Admin come back as before.

### Things to know

- **A page only shows what you can open right now.** A feature your firm hides
  or your account doesn't have is left off the page (and off its pills); the
  editor still lists it so it comes back if the feature does.
- Deleting a page takes its own tiles with it (nothing else is deleted:
  the features are still in All). Deleting a button takes it off every page
  that held it (see [Deleting a button](#deleting-a-button)).
- Your firm can make pages for everyone too, and can decide who sees each
  ([Firm Launcher](../05-Subsystems/14-firm-launcher.md#firm-pages)). A firm
  page you can see is a tab like your own; a firm page meant for other roles
  is simply not there for you.

## Code: running JavaScript

**Code** opens a box where you can paste or type JavaScript and run it inside
YisraCase. It is the browser's developer console without opening the
developer tools: handy when IT sends you a line to run, or for a quick
`await apiSend('/api/…')`.

- **Opening it.** Code is not on any page by default, because it is an
  advanced tool. Press **Ctrl+K** (**⌘K** on a Mac) and type *code*, or add
  it to your Home or sidebar, or give it a [keyboard shortcut](#keyboard-shortcuts).
- **It runs as you.** The code can do anything your login can do, and nothing
  more: every request it makes is checked against your account, the same as
  if you clicked through the screens yourself. That is also why it is open to
  everyone: it gives no one extra access.
- **Only run code from someone you trust at the firm.** Never paste code sent
  to you in an email or a text, found on a website, or read to you by a
  caller. Someone asking you to do that is trying to scam you: code run here
  can read your login and send it, or your clients' data, anywhere.
- **Every run is recorded** (who ran it, when, and the code itself) before it
  runs. If the record can't be saved, the code does not run.
- **Running.** Press **Run**, or **Ctrl+Enter** (**⌘+Enter** on a Mac).
  Nothing runs when you paste. A single expression shows its value
  (`1 + 1` shows `2`); longer code shows whatever it `return`s. You can use
  `await`. **Copy output** copies the whole result.
- **Things to know.** The box always opens empty, and closing it clears it.
  A click outside the box does not close it; **Esc**, **Close** and **×** do.
  Variables don't carry over from one run to the next (store one on
  `window` to keep it). Code that is already running can't be stopped, the
  same as in the console. Pop-up messages the code opens (alerts,
  confirmations, toasts) appear on top of the box.

## What your firm controls

Your firm's launcher settings (More → YisraCase Config → Firm, see
[Firm Launcher](../05-Subsystems/14-firm-launcher.md)) apply underneath your
arrangement:

- **Your Home starts from the firm's.** Your firm can arrange Home, the
  sidebar and the small buttons under the Home tiles for everyone (and the More
  Features and Admin pages, and its own pages). Until you arrange a surface yourself, you see the
  firm's arrangement, and the editor starts from it. Once you have arranged
  one, a later change by the firm only **adds** new items for you, next to
  their neighbours: it never reorders or takes away yours. **Reset** brings
  back the firm's arrangement.
- **Firm pages.** A page your firm made shows as a tab for everyone the firm
  lets see it (**Who sees what**). You can put a firm page on your Home or
  sidebar, in a list or on your own page, like any item. The firm arranges its
  tiles, and you can rearrange them for yourself (see
  [Your firm's pages](#your-firms-pages)).

- **Firm hides.** A feature the firm hid is gone from every surface, from your
  layout, from your lists and pages and from **Add a shortcut**. If the firm brings it back, so does your
  launcher: it comes back where you had it (or, if you never had it, in its
  usual place).
- **Renames.** A feature the firm renamed shows the firm's name in your
  editor, on your shortcuts and in your keyboard shortcut list too.
- **Locks.** A locked item shows **Locked**. You can move it, but you can't
  hide it from a surface it is on by default.
- **Firm items.** The firm's own links, tools and saved views can be pinned to
  your Home or sidebar like any feature, from **Add a shortcut**.
- **Your own buttons** (see [My buttons](#my-buttons)) are not touched by any
  of the above: the firm can't hide, rename or lock them.
- A feature that isn't available to your account never appears, here or
  anywhere else. Hiding is about tidiness, not access: what you may open is
  decided by your role, whatever the launcher shows.

## Not here (yet)

- **More Features and Admin** keep the order your firm and the app give them.
  Only Home, the sidebar and your own pages are yours to arrange.
- Firm-wide keyboard shortcuts: shortcuts are personal only.
- **Your own page in the address bar.** Reloading while on one of your pages
  lands on Home, and a link to one can't be shared or bookmarked. (Your
  firm's pages do come back: see [Your workspace in the address
  bar](14-workspace-url.md).)
