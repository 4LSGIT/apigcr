# Your own launcher: All, Ctrl+K, Home and the sidebar, and keyboard shortcuts

The **launcher** is every way into a feature: the sidebar, the Home tiles, the
More Features and Admin grids, the **All** tab and the **Ctrl+K** search. Your
firm sets it up for everyone. On top of that, you can arrange **your own Home
tiles and your own sidebar**, and give features **keyboard shortcuts** of
your own. Nobody else sees your arrangement or your shortcuts, and both are
saved on your account, so they follow you to any computer you sign in on.

---

## All and Ctrl+K: every feature, always

**All**, the last item in the sidebar and the last tile on Home, lists every
feature your account has, with a search box on top. **Ctrl+K** (**⌘K** on a
Mac) opens the same search from anywhere. See *Finding any feature* in the
[introduction](01-introduction.md).

Whatever you hide or move, **All and Ctrl+K still list everything you have**.
All itself can't be hidden or moved, so it is always there to find a feature
and to undo a change.

## Customizing Home and the sidebar

Open **All** and press **Customize my launcher** (under the search box). The
editor opens over the app: a card on a computer, the whole screen on a phone.
It starts from your layout as it is saved right now, so a change you saved
from another browser tab or computer is already there (your Home and sidebar
catch up to it as the editor opens). If it can't load your layout, it says
*Couldn't load your current layout; try again* beside the button and doesn't
open.

The editor has two tabs at the top: **Home & sidebar**, described here, and
**Keyboard shortcuts** (see [Keyboard shortcuts](#keyboard-shortcuts) below).
A dot on a tab means it has unsaved changes.

**Home & sidebar** has two sections, **Home** and **Sidebar**. Each one lists that surface
exactly as you see it now, top to bottom: features with their icon and name,
**Row break** rows on Home, and section headings (like *Records*) on the
sidebar. Each row has buttons on the right:

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **↑ / ↓** | Moves the row up or down one place. On Home, the order is the tile order, left to right and row by row. |
| **Eye with a slash** (Hide) | On a standard item: takes its shortcut off this surface. |
| **×** (Remove) | On something you added (a shortcut, a row break, a section label): takes it out. |
| **Lock** (greyed) | Your firm locked this item: you can move it, but not hide it. |

Some rows carry a small tag: **Added** (a shortcut you added), **Firm** (one
of your firm's own items) or **Locked**.

A section heading on the sidebar is edited in place: click into its box and
type. A heading is plain text, 1 to 40 characters.

### Adding

- **Add a shortcut** opens a list of every feature you have that isn't on
  that surface yet, grouped the way All groups them, with a search box. Pick
  one and it goes to the end of the list; move it with the arrows. Anything
  can go on Home or the sidebar: a More Features or Admin panel, a firm item,
  a sidebar feature on Home. A shortcut does exactly what the feature does
  anywhere else. A More panel pinned to Home, for example, opens More Features
  with that panel showing.
- **Add a row break** (Home) starts a new row of tiles.
- **Add a section label** (sidebar) adds a heading. Type it and press
  **Add** or **Enter**.

## Hide versus remove

- **Hide** is for the standard items, the ones a surface has by default.
  Hiding one takes it off **that surface only**: hiding Billing from the
  sidebar leaves its Home tile alone. Hidden items are listed under **Hidden
  on this surface** at the end of the section, each with a **Show** button
  that puts it back in its usual place.
- **Remove** is for what you added yourself. It's simply gone; you can add it
  again from **Add a shortcut**.
- Neither one takes a feature away from you. It's still in All and Ctrl+K,
  and it still opens from anywhere else it appears.

When YisraCase adds a new feature to Home or the sidebar, it shows up for you
too, next to the item it sits beside by default, even if you've rearranged
that surface. You can then move it or hide it like any other.

## Saving and cancelling

- **Nothing changes until you press Save.** One Save covers both tabs. It
  updates your Home, your sidebar and your keyboard shortcuts straight away,
  with no reload, and closes the editor. The status
  line at the bottom says *Unsaved changes* or *No changes*; Save is greyed
  out while there is nothing to save.
- **Cancel**, the **×** at the top, **Esc** and a click outside the card all
  close the editor. With unsaved changes they ask first, and so does
  reloading the page.
- If a save is refused, the reason shows at the top of the editor, and under
  the section it is about. Your changes stay in the editor; fix them, or
  Cancel. When both tabs had changes, each is saved on its own: the message
  says which one was saved and which wasn't, the one that was saved is done
  (its tab loses its dot), and the other keeps your changes for another try.
- If you have YisraCase open in more than one browser tab, the others pick up
  your arrangement the next time they load, or when you open the editor there.
  If the editor is open in two tabs at once and you save from both, the later
  save replaces the earlier one.
- While a save is on its way, the editor takes no further changes.

## Reset

**Reset Home to default** and **Reset sidebar to default** each put **one**
surface back to the standard layout. They ask first, and take effect when you
Save. The other surface keeps your arrangement.

## Keyboard shortcuts

A keyboard shortcut opens a feature from anywhere in YisraCase with a key
combination you choose: **⌃⌘T** (Control + Command + T) on a Mac, say, or
**Ctrl+Alt+T** on Windows, for Tasks. It works wherever you are in the app,
even while you are typing in a field or inside a page like a case, and does
exactly what clicking the feature does. The **Ctrl+K** search shows each
feature's shortcut beside its name.

### Adding one

1. Open **All**, press **Customize my launcher**, and pick the **Keyboard
   shortcuts** tab.
2. Press **Add a keyboard shortcut** and find the feature (type part of its
   name, or pick it from the list). Any feature you have can get one,
   including More Features and Admin panels and your firm's own items.
3. Click into the **keys** box and press the combination. The box shows what
   you pressed and says straight away whether it is free, taken or blocked.
   **Backspace** clears it.
4. Press **Add shortcut** (or **Enter**), then **Save**.

The list shows each shortcut with its feature. **×** removes one. A feature
can have more than one shortcut, but a combination can open only one feature:
the editor says which feature already has it.

### Which combinations you can't use, and why

- **A combination needs Ctrl, ⌘ (Command) or Alt (⌥ Option).** Without one,
  it would go off while you type. Shift alone doesn't count.
- **Ctrl+K and ⌘K** open the search, so they can't be shortcuts.
- **Ctrl or ⌘ with just one key** (⌘T, Ctrl+W, ⌘C, Ctrl+F…) belongs to the
  browser or to typing: new tab, close, reload, the address bar, copy, paste,
  undo, find, print. Some of those the browser never lets a page have at all.
  Add a second modifier instead: the editor suggests **⌃⌘** plus your key on a
  Mac and **Ctrl+Alt** plus your key elsewhere.
- **Alt (⌥) on its own** types special characters on a Mac (⌥E makes é) and
  opens the browser's menus on Windows.
- **Tab and Esc** stay free for moving between fields and closing dialogs.
- A few combinations the computer itself uses: for example ⌃⌘F (full screen),
  ⌃⌘Q (lock screen) and ⌃⌘Space (emoji) on a Mac, ⌘⌥I and its neighbours (the
  browser's developer tools), and Ctrl+Alt+Delete on Windows.

The editor names the reason whenever it refuses a combination.

**A warning, not a block: Ctrl+Alt on Windows.** On keyboards with an
**AltGr** key, which most non-US Windows layouts have, Ctrl+Alt with a key can
type a character such as € or @. The editor warns you when you pick a
Ctrl+Alt combination. If typing that character stops working in YisraCase,
pick another combination.

### Things to know

- **Shortcuts follow you.** They are saved on your account, so they work on
  any computer you sign in on. A shortcut that uses ⌘ needs a Mac keyboard;
  if you also use Windows, Ctrl+Alt combinations work on both.
- **A shortcut to something you can't see does nothing.** If your firm hides
  the feature, or it isn't available to your account, pressing the shortcut
  does nothing at all. The editor lists it as **Unavailable** and keeps it,
  so it works again if the feature comes back; remove it with **×** if you
  don't want it.
- **Shortcuts need a keyboard.** On a phone you can see and remove them in the
  editor, but not press them.
- They don't fire while a dialog is open, while this editor is open, or before
  you sign in. An editor that uses a combination for itself (an email editor's
  own shortcuts, for example) keeps it.
- Holding a shortcut down opens the feature once.

## What your firm controls

Your firm's launcher settings (More → YisraCase Config → Firm, see
[Firm Launcher](../05-Subsystems/14-firm-launcher.md)) apply underneath your
arrangement:

- **Firm hides.** A feature the firm hid is gone from every surface, from your
  layout and from **Add a shortcut**. If the firm brings it back, so does your
  launcher: it comes back where you had it (or, if you never had it, in its
  usual place).
- **Renames.** A feature the firm renamed shows the firm's name in your
  editor, on your shortcuts and in your keyboard shortcut list too.
- **Locks.** A locked item shows **Locked**. You can move it, but you can't
  hide it from a surface it is on by default.
- **Firm items.** The firm's own links, tools and saved views can be pinned to
  your Home or sidebar like any feature, from **Add a shortcut**.
- A feature that isn't available to your account never appears, here or
  anywhere else. Hiding is about tidiness, not access: what you may open is
  decided by your role, whatever the launcher shows.

## Not here (yet)

- **More Features and Admin** keep the order your firm and the app give them.
  Only Home and the sidebar are yours to arrange.
- Firm-wide keyboard shortcuts: shortcuts are personal only.
