# Your own launcher: All, Ctrl+K, Home and the sidebar, keyboard shortcuts, and your own buttons

The **launcher** is every way into a feature: the sidebar, the Home tiles, the
More Features and Admin grids, the **All** tab and the **Ctrl+K** search. Your
firm sets it up for everyone. On top of that, you can arrange **your own Home
tiles and your own sidebar**, give features **keyboard shortcuts** of your
own, and make **buttons of your own** (a saved view, a tool, a link to
another website, or a **drawer** that holds several). Nobody else sees your arrangement, your shortcuts or your
buttons, and all three are saved on your account, so they follow you to any
computer you sign in on.

---

## All and Ctrl+K: every feature, always

**All**, the last item in the sidebar and the last tile on Home, lists every
feature your account has, with a search box on top. **Ctrl+K** (**⌘K** on a
Mac) opens the same search from anywhere. See *Finding any feature* in the
[introduction](01-introduction.md).

Whatever you hide or move, **All and Ctrl+K still list everything you have**.
All itself can't be hidden or moved, so it is always there to find a feature
and to undo a change.

## Customizing Home and the sidebar

Open **All** and press **Customize my launcher** (under the search box). The
editor opens over the app: a card on a computer, the whole screen on a phone.
It starts from your layout as it is saved right now, so a change you saved
from another browser tab or computer is already there (your Home and sidebar
catch up to it as the editor opens). If it can't load your layout, it says
*Couldn't load your current layout; try again* beside the button and doesn't
open.

The editor has three tabs at the top: **Home & sidebar**, described here,
**Keyboard shortcuts** (see [Keyboard shortcuts](#keyboard-shortcuts) below)
and **My buttons** (see [My buttons](#my-buttons) below). A dot on a tab means
it has unsaved changes.

**Home & sidebar** has two sections, **Home** and **Sidebar**. Each one lists that surface
exactly as you see it now, top to bottom: features with their icon and name,
**Row break** rows on Home, and section headings (like *Records*) on the
sidebar. Each row has buttons on the right:

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **↑ / ↓** | Moves the row up or down one place. On Home, the order is the tile order, left to right and row by row. |
| **Eye with a slash** (Hide) | On a standard item: takes its shortcut off this surface. |
| **×** (Remove) | On something you added (a shortcut, a row break, a section label): takes it out. |
| **Lock** (greyed) | Your firm locked this item: you can move it, but not hide it. |

Some rows carry a small tag: **Added** (a shortcut you added), **Firm** (one
of your firm's own items) or **Locked**.

A section heading on the sidebar is edited in place: click into its box and
type. A heading is plain text, 1 to 40 characters.

### Adding

- **Add a shortcut** opens a list of every feature you have that isn't on
  that surface yet, grouped the way All groups them, with a search box. Pick
  one and it goes to the end of the list; move it with the arrows. Anything
  can go on Home or the sidebar: a More Features or Admin panel, a firm item,
  a sidebar feature on Home. A shortcut does exactly what the feature does
  anywhere else. A More panel pinned to Home, for example, opens More Features
  with that panel showing.
- **Add a row break** (Home) starts a new row of tiles.
- **Add a section label** (sidebar) adds a heading. Type it and press
  **Add** or **Enter**.

## Hide versus remove

- **Hide** is for the standard items, the ones a surface has by default.
  Hiding one takes it off **that surface only**: hiding Billing from the
  sidebar leaves its Home tile alone. Hidden items are listed under **Hidden
  on this surface** at the end of the section, each with a **Show** button
  that puts it back in its usual place.
- **Remove** is for what you added yourself. It's simply gone; you can add it
  again from **Add a shortcut**.
- Neither one takes a feature away from you. It's still in All and Ctrl+K,
  and it still opens from anywhere else it appears.

When YisraCase adds a new feature to Home or the sidebar, it shows up for you
too, next to the item it sits beside by default, even if you've rearranged
that surface. You can then move it or hide it like any other.

## Saving and cancelling

- **Nothing changes until you press Save.** One Save covers all three tabs.
  It updates your Home, your sidebar, your keyboard shortcuts and your buttons
  straight away, with no reload, and closes the editor. The status
  line at the bottom says *Unsaved changes* or *No changes*; Save is greyed
  out while there is nothing to save.
- **Cancel**, the **×** at the top, **Esc** and a click outside the card all
  close the editor. With unsaved changes they ask first, and so does
  reloading the page.
- If a save is refused, the reason shows at the top of the editor, and under
  the section it is about. Your changes stay in the editor; fix them, or
  Cancel. When more than one tab had changes, each is saved on its own, your
  buttons first: the message says which were saved and which weren't, the
  ones that were saved are done (their tabs lose their dot), and the others
  keep your changes for another try. If your buttons don't save, a Home,
  sidebar or shortcut change that uses a **new** button isn't sent either (it
  would point at a button that doesn't exist yet); it waits for your next Save.
- If you have YisraCase open in more than one browser tab, the others pick up
  your arrangement the next time they load, or when you open the editor there.
  If the editor is open in two tabs at once and you save from both, the later
  save replaces the earlier one.
- While a save is on its way, the editor takes no further changes.

## Reset

**Reset Home to default** and **Reset sidebar to default** each put **one**
surface back to the standard layout. They ask first, and take effect when you
Save. The other surface keeps your arrangement.

## Keyboard shortcuts

A keyboard shortcut opens a feature from anywhere in YisraCase with a key
combination you choose: **⌃⌘T** (Control + Command + T) on a Mac, say, or
**Ctrl+Alt+T** on Windows, for Tasks. It works wherever you are in the app,
even while you are typing in a field or inside a page like a case, and does
exactly what clicking the feature does. The **Ctrl+K** search shows each
feature's shortcut beside its name.

### Adding one

1. Open **All**, press **Customize my launcher**, and pick the **Keyboard
   shortcuts** tab.
2. Press **Add a keyboard shortcut** and find the feature (type part of its
   name, or pick it from the list). Any feature you have can get one,
   including More Features and Admin panels and your firm's own items.
3. Click into the **keys** box and press the combination. The box shows what
   you pressed and says straight away whether it is free, taken or blocked.
   **Backspace** clears it.
4. Press **Add shortcut** (or **Enter**), then **Save**.

The list shows each shortcut with its feature. **×** removes one. A feature
can have more than one shortcut, but a combination can open only one feature:
the editor says which feature already has it.

### Which combinations you can't use, and why

- **A combination needs Ctrl, ⌘ (Command) or Alt (⌥ Option).** Without one,
  it would go off while you type. Shift alone doesn't count.
- **Ctrl+K and ⌘K** open the search, so they can't be shortcuts.
- **Ctrl or ⌘ with just one key** (⌘T, Ctrl+W, ⌘C, Ctrl+F…) belongs to the
  browser or to typing: new tab, close, reload, the address bar, copy, paste,
  undo, find, print. Some of those the browser never lets a page have at all.
  Add a second modifier instead: the editor suggests **⌃⌘** plus your key on a
  Mac and **Ctrl+Alt** plus your key elsewhere.
- **Alt (⌥) on its own** types special characters on a Mac (⌥E makes é) and
  opens the browser's menus on Windows.
- **Tab and Esc** stay free for moving between fields and closing dialogs.
- A few combinations the computer itself uses: for example ⌃⌘F (full screen),
  ⌃⌘Q (lock screen) and ⌃⌘Space (emoji) on a Mac, ⌘⌥I and its neighbours (the
  browser's developer tools), and Ctrl+Alt+Delete on Windows.

The editor names the reason whenever it refuses a combination.

**A warning, not a block: Ctrl+Alt on Windows.** On keyboards with an
**AltGr** key, which most non-US Windows layouts have, Ctrl+Alt with a key can
type a character such as € or @. The editor warns you when you pick a
Ctrl+Alt combination. If typing that character stops working in YisraCase,
pick another combination.

### Things to know

- **Shortcuts follow you.** They are saved on your account, so they work on
  any computer you sign in on. A shortcut that uses ⌘ needs a Mac keyboard;
  if you also use Windows, Ctrl+Alt combinations work on both.
- **A shortcut to something you can't see does nothing.** If your firm hides
  the feature, or it isn't available to your account, pressing the shortcut
  does nothing at all. The editor lists it as **Unavailable** and keeps it,
  so it works again if the feature comes back; remove it with **×** if you
  don't want it.
- **Shortcuts need a keyboard.** On a phone you can see and remove them in the
  editor, but not press them.
- They don't fire while a dialog is open, while this editor is open, or before
  you sign in. An editor that uses a combination for itself (an email editor's
  own shortcuts, for example) keeps it.
- Holding a shortcut down opens the feature once.

## My buttons

Your own buttons, besides the features and your firm's items: a saved view
you use every day, a tool, the court's website. Make up to **50**. Only you
see them, and they are saved on your account like the rest of this page.

### What a button can open

| Kind | What it opens | How |
|---|---|---|
| **A saved view** | One of the firm's saved views (picked from a list) | Over the app, in a window you close with **×** or **Esc**. Pick **Large** (the usual) or **Compact** (a small card). |
| **A tool** | One of the firm's live tools (picked from a list) | The same way: over the app, Large or Compact. |
| **A link to a website** | Any secure address (`https://…`), or a page of YisraCase itself (`/…`) | **Always in a new browser window**, never inside YisraCase. |
| **A drawer** | A small menu of up to 30 other items: features, your firm's items, your other buttons | Beside its tile or sidebar row; pick an item to open it. See [Drawers](#drawers). |

**Why links open in a new window.** Another website isn't part of YisraCase:
shown inside the app, it would look like YisraCase while being somebody
else's page, and many sites refuse to be shown inside another one anyway. A
new window makes plain where you are. For the same reason a link must be
`https://`, and it can't point at a page that only works inside YisraCase
(a tool's or a saved view's own page): make a **Tool** or **Saved view**
button for those.

### Making one

1. Open **All**, press **Customize my launcher**, and pick the **My buttons**
   tab.
2. Press **Add a button** and fill in:
   - **Name**: what the button says, 1 to 40 characters;
   - **Icon**: press **Choose…** and pick one (see
     [Choosing an icon](#choosing-an-icon)); the icon and its name show
     beside the button;
   - **Opens**: a saved view, a tool, or a link; then pick the view or the tool
     from the list (with its **Window size**), or type the address.
3. Press **Add button**, then **Save**.

Each button in the list shows its icon, its name, what it opens (*Saved view ·
Leads*, *Link · ecf.example.gov*), and where you have put it (*On Home*, *On
sidebar*, its keyboard shortcut). **↑ / ↓** set their order, which is their
order in All and the search. The **pen** edits one, the **bin** deletes it.
A button you haven't saved yet says **New**.

If the lists of tools and saved views can't be loaded, the tab says so: link
buttons still work, and the lists come back the next time you open the
editor.

### Choosing an icon

**Choose…** opens the icon picker: every free
[Font Awesome](https://fontawesome.com/icons) icon, about 2,000 of them, in
three families: **Solid** (filled), **Regular** (outlined; a smaller set) and
**Brands** (company logos, such as GitHub or Dropbox).

- **Search**: type part of a name or a related word (`calendar`, `money`,
  `github`). Older Font Awesome names find their new icon too (`home` finds
  *house*).
- **All / Solid / Regular / Brands** narrow the list to one family.
- The icon you have now is outlined, and is named at the top.
- Click an icon to pick it. Point at one (or move to it) to see its name.
- Keys: **↓** from the search box moves into the icons; the arrow keys,
  **Home** and **End** move around; **Enter** picks; **Enter** in the search
  box picks the first match; **Esc** or **Cancel** closes it and keeps the icon
  you had.

The same picker chooses the icon of your
[custom tab](../05-Subsystems/05-YisraView.md#your-own-tab) and of your firm's items
([Firm launcher](../05-Subsystems/14-firm-launcher.md)).

### Putting a button on Home or the sidebar, and giving it a shortcut

A button you make is in **All** and the **Ctrl+K** search straight away, under
**Mine**. To have it on your Home or sidebar, press **Add to Home** or **Add to
sidebar** under it in the list (or use **Add a shortcut** on the Home & sidebar
tab); then arrange it there like anything else. On the **Keyboard shortcuts**
tab you can give it a key combination of its own. All of this works before
you save: the button, its place and its shortcut are saved together.

### Deleting a button

The bin deletes a button. If it is on your Home or sidebar, in one of your
drawers, or has a keyboard shortcut, the editor says so and asks first;
deleting it takes it off those too, so nothing is left pointing at it. Nothing
is gone until you Save.

### Things to know

- **Your firm's settings don't reach your buttons.** The firm's hides,
  renames and **Who sees what** apply to the app's features and the firm's
  items; your own buttons are yours. A button is a shortcut, not a key: what
  the tool or view behind it lets you do is still decided by your role.
- A button's internal name (its id) is made from the name you give it and
  never changes once saved, so renaming a saved button keeps its place on
  your Home and its shortcut. A deleted button's id is never used again for
  you.
- Buttons that open as a tab of their own, like the custom tab in Settings,
  are not here yet.

## Drawers

A **drawer** is a button that holds other items: put the five things you open
every morning in one, give it a single tile on your Home or a single row on
your sidebar, and open the rest from there. It is one of your buttons, so it
counts toward the 50, is listed under **Mine** in All and the search, and can
go on Home, on the sidebar, and get a keyboard shortcut like any other.

### Making one

1. On the **My buttons** tab, press **Add a button**, give it a **Name** and an
   **Icon**, and set **Opens** to **A drawer**.
2. Press **Add to the drawer** and pick from the list (type part of a name to
   find it). Anything you have can go in: a feature, a More Features or Admin
   panel, one of your firm's items, or one of your own buttons, even one you
   made a minute ago and haven't saved. The list stays open, so you can pick
   several in a row; **Done** closes it.
3. **↑ / ↓** set the order the drawer shows them in, and **×** takes one out.
   A drawer holds up to **30** items.
4. Press **Add button**, then **Save**. Put it on your Home or sidebar with
   **Add to Home** / **Add to sidebar**, as with any button.

In the list of buttons, a drawer says how many items it holds (*Drawer · 4
items*), and each button inside one is tagged with the drawer's name.

### Opening it

- Click its tile or sidebar row: a small menu opens right beside it. On a
  phone it slides up from the bottom of the screen.
- From **All**, it opens under the tile you clicked; from the **Ctrl+K**
  search or its keyboard shortcut, near the top of the screen.
- Click an item to open it; the drawer closes as it does.
- **Esc**, the **×**, or a click anywhere outside closes it, and so does a
  second click on its tile. With the keyboard, **↑ / ↓** (and **Home** /
  **End**) move between the items and **Enter** opens one.
- Only one drawer is open at a time. It doesn't open while a dialog or the
  **Customize my launcher** editor is open.

### Things to know

- **A drawer only shows what you can open right now.** If your firm hides a
  feature you put in a drawer, or it isn't available to your account, the
  drawer simply leaves it out (the editor still lists it, tagged
  **Unavailable**, so it comes back if the feature does). A drawer with
  nothing you can open says *Nothing here you can open*.
- **One level deep.** A drawer can't hold another drawer, and a button that is
  inside a drawer can't be turned into one; take it out of that drawer first.
- Deleting a button takes it out of every drawer that held it (see
  [Deleting a button](#deleting-a-button)).

## What your firm controls

Your firm's launcher settings (More → YisraCase Config → Firm, see
[Firm Launcher](../05-Subsystems/14-firm-launcher.md)) apply underneath your
arrangement:

- **Firm hides.** A feature the firm hid is gone from every surface, from your
  layout, from your drawers and from **Add a shortcut**. If the firm brings it back, so does your
  launcher: it comes back where you had it (or, if you never had it, in its
  usual place).
- **Renames.** A feature the firm renamed shows the firm's name in your
  editor, on your shortcuts and in your keyboard shortcut list too.
- **Locks.** A locked item shows **Locked**. You can move it, but you can't
  hide it from a surface it is on by default.
- **Firm items.** The firm's own links, tools and saved views can be pinned to
  your Home or sidebar like any feature, from **Add a shortcut**.
- **Your own buttons** (see [My buttons](#my-buttons)) are not touched by any
  of the above: the firm can't hide, rename or lock them.
- A feature that isn't available to your account never appears, here or
  anywhere else. Hiding is about tidiness, not access: what you may open is
  decided by your role, whatever the launcher shows.

## Not here (yet)

- **More Features and Admin** keep the order your firm and the app give them.
  Only Home and the sidebar are yours to arrange.
- Firm-wide keyboard shortcuts: shortcuts are personal only.
